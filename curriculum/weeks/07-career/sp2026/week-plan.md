# Week 7: Career Week (Spring 2026)

## Philosophy

You've spent six weeks building things. Now it's time to make sure the world knows who you are and what you can do.

This is not a passive week. You're not sitting in lectures about resume formatting. You're doing real career work — figuring out who you are, telling that story clearly, reaching out to real people, and building something targeted at a specific opportunity. By Saturday, you should have a complete, coherent professional presence, a network you've actively engaged, and a spearfish project that demonstrates what you can do for a specific company or role.

The arc of the week: **Know yourself → Market yourself → Connect with people → Target an opportunity → Show your work.**

---

## Monday: Foundation

**The point:** Before you can market yourself, you need to know what you're marketing. Today is about honest self-reflection, getting your story straight, and auditing every surface where you show up professionally.

### What You'll Do

1. **Journal** — Spend at least an hour writing honestly about: who you are, what you can do, what you can offer right now, where you want to go, how you want to grow, and where you belong.
2. **Elevator Pitch** — Distill your journal into four versions of your pitch: 5 seconds, 30 seconds, 60 seconds, and 5 minutes.
3. **Audit** — Review every professional surface (LinkedIn, personal website, resume, GitHub) and mark each item as Good, Gap, or Misaligned.
4. **Close the Gaps** — Start fixing what's broken. Every surface should tell the same story.

### Artifact

Career starter repo with journal, elevator pitch, and audit committed.

See [Day 1 lesson plan](day-01-monday.md) for the full guide.

## Tuesday: Know Thyself, Market Thyself, Refine

**The point:** Yesterday was about career framing. Today gets personal. You are a specific person — figure out who that is, and then tell people who you are. Then use AI and human feedback to pressure-test everything.

### Three Parts

1. **Know Thyself** — Go deeper than yesterday's career framing. The personal part. What are you actually interested in? What makes you *you*, beyond your resume? This is the raw material that makes your marketing authentic rather than generic.

2. **Market Thyself** — Translate self-knowledge into material improvements across all your artifacts. Start with your personal website — it's the complete expression of you. Then work through every surface: blog posts, about pages, LinkedIn, resume, GitHub profile. The question for each one: *How can I communicate who I actually am?*

3. **Refine** — Feedback loop on every document and profile:
   - **Autonomous feedback:** Run each artifact through Claude using the review prompts provided. Get feedback from multiple AI personas (recruiter, hiring manager).
   - **Human feedback:** It is your responsibility to get at least one human review on every artifact. Valid sources: instructors, peers you respect, colleagues in industry. We are not going to chase you on this — but if you show up to a coaching meeting and haven't had anyone review your materials, that's on you.

### Artifact

Updated artifacts across all surfaces, with documented feedback received.

See [Day 2 lesson plan](day-02-tuesday.md) for prompts and detailed instructions.

## Wednesday: Tap Your Network

**The point:** Your next opportunity is probably going to come from another person. Today you start talking to people — not in a cringe, transactional networking way, but genuinely connecting with people you want to talk to.

### The Challenge: 100 Conversations

Your goal is to talk to 100 people. You will not hit 100 today — this is a quest that extends beyond this week. But you're starting today, and you're going to track every single one.

**Why 100?** It's probabilistic. By the time you reach 100 conversations, you'll have a dramatically different perspective on how to go about finding opportunities. You'll also have way more contact with reality than you'd get from endlessly polishing your LinkedIn profile.

### The Process

1. **Identify your network.** Stream-of-consciousness list every single person who might have any reason to help you, who's in a position to help you, who you can identify by name. Cast the net wide.
2. **Filter by warmth and excitement.** Of those people — who has even the smallest reason to help you? Who are you genuinely excited to talk to? Start with the warmest leads.
3. **Craft genuine asks.** This is a design process:
   - First, write the most vulnerable, truthful version of what you want to say. You don't need to send this — but writing it gets you to what's real.
   - Then flip perspective. Empathize with the recipient. What would *delight* this person to hear from you?
   - Craft the actual message from that place of honesty + empathy.
4. **Send messages. Have conversations. Repeat.**

### 10 Daily Career Actions

Aim for at least 10 career-related actions per day: messages sent, applications submitted, conversations had, connections made. Log every single one — who you talked to, what you said, what happened.

### Mandatory Celebration

This is not a chore. This is an opportunity. You live in an insane, complex, amazing world where you get to talk to people about incredible things. When you take actions, celebrate them. If you don't, the process will start to feel like a grind and you'll stop. Log your wins. If you didn't make progress today, that's fine — just log it honestly and move on.

### Artifact

Career action log: a running document (spreadsheet or markdown) tracking every outreach, conversation, and action — including the actual messages you sent.

## Thursday: Spearfishing (Day 1)

**The point:** Broad networking is about creating opportunities. Spearfishing is about targeting a specific one. Pick a company or role you're excited about, and build something that demonstrates exactly why they should hire you.

### What Is a Spearfish?

A spearfish is a targeted project — something you build specifically to show a particular company or team that you can do the work they need done. It's not a generic portfolio piece. It's a love letter to a specific opportunity.

### The Process

1. **Pick your target.** Which company, team, or role? Why them? What do they care about?
2. **Research.** What are their problems? What technology do they use? What would make them stop scrolling and pay attention?
3. **Build.** Create something that speaks directly to their needs. This could be a tool, a demo, a prototype, a contribution to their open-source work — whatever makes the strongest case that you understand their world and can add value in it.

### Keep Networking

Don't stop your daily career actions. Keep logging. Keep reaching out. Spearfishing and networking run in parallel.

### Artifact

Spearfish project in progress. Continue updating your career action log.

## Saturday: Career Report & Peer Review

**The point:** This is not a traditional demo day. Instead of presenting what you built, you're presenting proof that you showed up this week — every action you took, every message you sent, every surface you improved. The artifact is the process, not just the product.

### The Report

Prepare a printed report that includes:

1. **All your profiles** — QR codes linking to your LinkedIn, personal website, GitHub, resume, and any other professional surfaces. Peers and instructors will review these async and give in-depth feedback.
2. **Your career action log** — Every outreach, conversation, and action you took this week. Include the actual messages you sent. Funnel data if you have it: how many people you contacted, how many responded, how many conversations happened, how far down the pipeline you got.
3. **Your spearfish** — Include as a QR code or link on your report. The video walkthrough is the demo.
4. **Graphs and data** — Visualize your activity. Show the volume. This is proof of work.

### The Session

- **Present your report.** Walk through the actions you took this week. This is a celebration of the work, not a defense of it.
- **Peer review profiles.** Review each other's professional surfaces in depth. This is the kind of feedback you can't get from a 5-minute demo — an hour-long conversation about how someone shows up in the world.
- **Give and receive feedback on outreach.** The messages you sent, the asks you made, how you approached people. This data is incredibly valuable — it's the complete representation of your process, and it's the thing that's hardest to get feedback on normally.

### Why This Format

Tech demos are great when you're showing off something you're proud of for its own sake. But this week is about career work, and the artifact that matters most is evidence that you're taking it seriously. A printout of all the actions you took, the messages you sent, and the profiles you built — that's what hunger looks like.

### Artifact

Printed career report. All profiles reviewed by at least one peer.
