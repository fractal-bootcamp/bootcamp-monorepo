# Day 6: Career Report & Peer Review

This is not demo day. You're not presenting a project — you're presenting proof that you showed up this week. Every action you took, every message you sent, every surface you improved. The artifact is the process, not just the product. You'll assemble a printed career report this morning, then break into small groups and spend real time reviewing each other's profiles, outreach, and spearfish work.

### Print It

The report should be printed — physical paper. This isn't arbitrary. A printed report forces you to commit to a final version. It's also easier to review in a small group than passing a laptop around. Print enough copies for your group (3-4 copies).

If you can't print, have a clean PDF ready to AirDrop or share.

## Part 2: Group Review

You'll break into groups of 3-4. Each person gets a dedicated block of time — roughly 20-30 minutes per person. Plan for about 1-1.5 hours total. This is not a 5-minute presentation followed by polite applause. It's a deep review. The small group format means everyone gets real time and real feedback.

Before you start: decide how your group will capture feedback. A shared Google doc per person works well — everyone can type comments in real time and the person keeps the doc after. Alternatively, mark up the printed reports directly. Whatever you choose, set it up now so you're not fumbling with logistics during the review.

### The Structure (Per Person)

**1. Walk through your report (5 minutes)**

Hit the highlights. Don't read the whole thing — your group has copies. Cover:

- How many career actions you took this week, and what the funnel looks like
- What you learned — about yourself, about the market, about your outreach
- What's working and what isn't
- Your spearfish: who it's for, what you built, and play the video (or have them scan the QR code)

This is a celebration of the work, not a defense of it. You showed up. Walk them through what that looked like.

**2. Review profiles (10 minutes)**

Your group scans the QR codes on your report and reviews your professional surfaces — LinkedIn, personal website, GitHub, resume — right there, on their phones or laptops.

What reviewers should look for:

- **First impression.** You have 10 seconds. What do you notice? What's confusing? What's missing?
- **Story coherence.** Does the same story come through on every surface? Or does LinkedIn say one thing and the website say another?
- **Specific feedback.** Not "looks good" — that's useless. "Your LinkedIn headline says X but your website says Y" is useful. "Your GitHub has no pinned repos" is useful. "Your resume buries the most impressive thing in the third bullet of your second role" is useful.
- **The recruiter test.** If I were a recruiter who found you on LinkedIn, would I click through to your website? Would I forward your profile to a hiring manager? Why or why not?

Give feedback out loud _and_ in the shared doc or on the printout. Written feedback is more useful — people forget verbal feedback by the time they get home.

**3. Review outreach (10 minutes)**

Go through the actual messages in the career action log. This is the feedback that's hardest to get normally — and the most valuable.

What reviewers should look for:

- **Would you respond to this?** Read each message as if you were the recipient. Be honest.
- **Is it specific?** Does it feel written for this person, or could it have gone to 50 people?
- **Is the ask clear?** After reading, does the recipient know what to do next?
- **What's the conversion rate?** Look at the funnel. If messages aren't converting to responses, why? Is it the message quality, the targeting, the channel, or just volume?
- **Follow-up patterns.** Did they follow up on non-responses? How many times? What did the follow-up say?

This is where the group earns its keep. Outreach is a skill, and the only way to get better at it is to have other people read your messages and tell you what's landing and what isn't.

### How to Give Good Feedback

- **Be specific.** "This is good" is not feedback. "This LinkedIn headline works because it positions you as X rather than a generic Y" is feedback.
- **Be honest.** If something is weak, say so. You're not helping anyone by being polite about a resume that needs work. The recruiter who sees it next won't be polite.
- **Be constructive.** Point out the problem AND suggest a fix. "Your outreach messages are too long" is half-useful. "Your outreach messages are too long — try cutting the first two sentences entirely, they're throat-clearing" is fully useful.
- **Write it down.** Use the shared doc or printout you set up at the start. Verbal feedback disappears — written feedback compounds.

## Part 3: After the Review

You just got more direct, specific feedback on your professional presence than most people get in a year. Use it.

### Immediate Actions (Today)

- [ ] Write down every piece of feedback you received — don't rely on memory
- [ ] Identify the top 3 changes that would make the biggest difference
- [ ] Make at least one fix today while the feedback is fresh

### This Weekend and Beyond

- [ ] Work through your feedback list and update every surface that needs it
- [ ] Send your spearfish email when it's ready (Monday morning is the sweet spot for cold outreach — don't send on a weekend)
- [ ] Keep logging career actions — the 100 Conversations Challenge doesn't stop because the week is over
- [ ] Follow up on every unanswered message from this week
- [ ] Plan your next spearfish — you can run this process again for a different company every few days

### The Bigger Picture

This week gave you a system, not a one-time exercise. The career action log, the daily targets, the outreach templates, the feedback loop, the spearfishing framework — these are all things you keep doing. The people who get hired aren't the ones who had the best Week 7. They're the ones who kept the momentum going in weeks 8, 9, 10, and beyond.

The 100 Conversations Challenge is still running. Keep counting.
